This tool, infinite-neck, helps you visualize notes across the neck of your instrument.

The fretboard is displayed as a spreadsheet-like table with cells representing each fret/string position. Clicking on cells puts "notes" on the virtual neck/fretboard, showing where you'll play them on the real fretboard of your instrument. You can add bars and beats, and loop the song. There's music theory baked into the tool, so you can automatically highlight all legal notes in G Mixolydian, for example, with separate sets of colors for chord notes, root, scale notes, passing-tones, and blue-notes.

infinite-neck has the following uses for practice and sharing:
✓ writing down new-to-you and interesting chord fingerings.
✓ capturing a concrete way to play a song.
✓ communicating the fabric of a sound: the possible notes and their moods.
✓ creating art with the patterns of notes, another way to internalize the layout and relationships of notes.
✓ improvising over the changes while looping.

You can assign colors and styles to notes.

For example: named notes (C#), chord notes (I, III, V), scale notes (natural minor), mode notes (Mixolydian), enclosure notes, or passing tones can all have different colors and styles so you can visualize these patterns up the neck.

You can create Sections, and Beats within Sections.

A Section is like a bar or multiple bars where the underlying notes, modes, and keys don't change. Within a section, a beat can highlight any notes. The next beat could highlight different notes, but "Named Notes" stay for the whole section.

Each new section gets: new keys, number of beats, caption, enharmonic sharps/flats.

That is, a Song has one or more Sections, and each Section has one or more Beats.

Each beat's length (time duration) is determined with the "BPM", or "Beats Per Minute" setting, and there can be any number of beats per section, from 1 to some large integer.

You can then loop over the Beats in one Section, or in all Sections.


Transport showing Section 1 of 5, on Beat 3 of 4.

You can save your songs to share with someone or to come back and practice them later.

Within Sections, one key is the root key for the band, and optionally, a separate root key for the "lead". ("Bill is playing F# over Dave and the band's B".)

To show four chord changes over, say, a Dmin mood, you could use one Section with four Beats, or you could use four Sections.

With four beats in one Section, the neck would look like, say, a sea of bluish notes with several notes highlighted in golden backlight.

  • the chord changes would be shown by MultiHighlight or by MidiHighlight, which highlight just the relevant notes across the neck(s) with golden box-shadows.
  • the band is in multiple blue colors, e.g. slightly different colors for the I, the V, the minor 3, the Seven.
  • these notes keep the same position and color for all beats in this Section.

Or, with four Sections, the neck would look like a sea of bluish notes, and as each section is played, you'd see notes change style and color, forming one chord per Section with their color changes, that are different than the Dmin mood, and so draw your eyes to the chosen notes/positions within a whole fabric of playable notes. You can also add beats (Highlights) to these Sections for more expression.

I find it essential, when communicating with musicians, to "think Piano". So in addition to the note markers, or fret positions that you should play, should not play, could play...etc., I have styled the guitar necks with piano key coloring embeded as ivory and ebony box inlay, though in software they look more like lit-up MIDI controllers built into your guitar neck like Guitar Hero with half of Synthesia bolted on.

That is, ahem, a Song has one or more Sections, and each Section has one or more Beats.

Why

Obviously, the neck is NOT infinite, since we play instruments with a fixed number of strings, and a playable number of frets, generating a listenable set of tones. (But you could have an infinite number of frets, since they get infinitely smaller as you approach the bridge, albeit infinitely more unplayable.)

And thinking about the neck as infinite helped me to learn the repeatable patterns of the neck, and not be rooted to the nut or open strings as much. In fact, I use open strings more now as drone notes while I'm playing high up on the neck, because going up the neck doesn't unroot me from the scale I'm playing.

The point is that the instrument that got me started on this madness was the Strandberg Boden 8-string guitar with 24 frets! It seemed infinite to me!

Also, deciding to play this beast meant that when switching to 8-string bass (or 5-string bass), I'd have to know different layouts than Standard guitar tuning. So I decided for my own practice, I would use Perfect-Fourth-Tuning (P4) on this 8-string guitar and all my basses. Soon, the logic and playability of this P4 system led me to convert some of my 6-string guitars to P4. My soloing and comping advanced remarkably. I never realized that the part of my brain that likes to improvise gets all anxious and goes away when I hit that major third between the G and B strings and I play a real clam because I'm missing a half-step.

Along the way, I had to sort out where my Classical training and Jazz training had different names for the pitches of a scale, and what to call these enharmonically, and what to call these pitches when the key changed, but the played note was the same.

  ☞  e.g. the band jumps from the key of A to the key of D# and you want to play the minor third function in the new key, or you want to know what that minor third you were playing in A means, now that everyone else is playing in D#. "Girl from Ipanema" anyone?

  ☞  Classical purists note--here we show your choice of [all sharps] or [all flats] in any key, per Section, but no weird enharmonic names or mixed sharps and flats--sorry, no B# or C## for you!

So I decided to share this tool and to include other tunings, and ambidexterity, so you can use this tool in your instrument and tuning of choice.

Open some of the example files under "File | Open song from: [Song Library]" to see it in action, or watch some of the videos on our youtube chanel.

You'll want to use this tool to:

  • Understand Laramie's system of scale note/function naming (a hybrid between Jazz and Classical conventions), and its uses for singing, playing, and visualizing modal and modeless playing.
  • Try the benefits of Ebony/Ivory visualization from the piano, brought to the guitar. Especially when learning modes or playing in "The People's Key".
  • Apprehend the patterns formed by scales and chords across and up the neck.
  • Move these patterns around the neck.
  • Watch lead patterns moving against patterns of comping, and practice improvising over the patterns and changes.
  • See the difference between tunings in real time (pull up two tunings and press Ctrl-minus in your browser to make them both fit).
  • Practice a song someone has saved in some other tuning, by observing it to your tuning (see listeners).

Classical music theory provides only 7 note names, and then expects you to glue on words like "augmented", "diminished", "altered", "major", and "minor". The problem is the symbols can change, so that for example the "II" becomes "ii" if diatonic, which makes it minor. Add Jazz lingo and you'll hear terms like "flat 9" or "the flat 2". And in Classical terms it would not be correct to notate a 12-tone chromatic scale thusly: I ii II iii III IV v V vi VI vii VII. Because ii represents the minor mode and not the flatted II. Modernized Solfège get close, but few people use this system, and it does not have single-character symbols. These examples don't explain the problems well, or even correctly, but that's kind of the point. These systems do not precisely address what I want to express: a unique set of single letter names for each of the twelve tones of the chromatic scale. I do this by assigning names concretely, which apply in any 12-tone playing, assuming you know where the "root" is, or the key of the band or dominant instrument, usually the bass player. I used symbols familiar to Jazz and Session players, and added a few new ones where singular, concrete names were needed.

Features of the system

  • The "Name" is my name for that symbol and function
  • The "Pronunciation" is my way of singing that symbol, for example "m" is named "mi" and is pronounced like the English word "my".
  • "Symbol" is also called Function, Function Name, or Note
  • A Θ is always 7 steps up from the root.
  • If you change root, the new Θ is calculated as 7 steps above the new root.
  • A III is always a major third.
  • An m is always a minor third, and so on.
  • It's easy to think of the notes as simple: one character, one syllable
  • I think the easiest to sing sounds are shown in my pronounciation--one syllable, ends in vowel.

Usage

The neck is shown as a table of cells, like a spreadsheet. A cell is one of the squares on the neck, and represents one fret on one string.

infinite-neck allows you to mark the notes on your Instrument with various types of notes, shown below. Otherwise, a cell is empty. (You can also choose to show all the note names in all cells, but these won't have an inner border or backglow behind them as notes do.) When a cell has one or more notes or highlights, it is called "Active".

With the various types of notes, you can express notes which should be known anywhere on the neck, or which should be played exactly where shown. With recording Beats and ordering the Sections, and with Looping, you can then arrange the song into a practice sheet, or a visualization of notes to play or notes you could play.

To help you visualize the neck, infinite-neck shows where the piano keys would lie on your tuning. In most Themes, cells have a white background for white keys on the piano, and a gray background for black keys on the piano.

Tunings may change on your guitar, such that a C may the the first fret on the second string, or it may be the open second string in P4. But a C is always a white key, and a C♯ (or D♭) is always a black key, except for these Themes:

  • "Coffy Brown" does not make a distinction between black and white.
  • "Blade" has black for black keys and hexagons in the white keys.
  • "Reverse" has white for the black keys, and black for the white keys.
  • "Snow" has white for all keys, with subtle grey borders.
  • "Wite-Out" has everything white and behaves as a completely blank canvas.
  • "Blackout" has all black keys.
  • "Matrix" has all black keys and command-line colors.
  • "Landau Top" has brown and tan keys (just like a cherry, stock Chevy Monte Carlo).

And any Theme can be changed by choosing the same color for noteWhiteKeyColor and noteBlackKeyColor in the Theme.

Startup

infinite-neck starts up with a blank song, and default instrument you can just start putting notes on by clicking in the cells that represent fret positions.

Once you have some notes you want to save as a song, use File | Download to save that song to your local computer. Later, you can open this song with File | Choose File. All the Sections, Instruments, and Notes, as well as the preferences and choices you make are saved in that song file, such as Tunings, Themes, View Options, and the Graveyard.

infinite-neck remembers just a few things between sessions that are not in the Default song. These you can set with the buttons on the Desktop page: Desktop | Save Scaling Prefs and Desktop | Save Instrument Prefs and apply to the Default song you get when you start up. Opening a song generally sets Scaling Prefs, and certainly has its own instruments displayed.

infinite-neck doesn't have many of the things we associate with computer programs. It doesn't do Copy/Paste, for example. It doesn't do Undo/Redo. But there are functions for getting what you need to get done, with different idioms.

Songfile Info

The Songfile Info page lets you keep notes right in the song file itself. This is handy for things like tuning reminders, setup notes, fingering hints, rehearsal directions, or a short formatted description of the song.

You can open Songfile Info in any of these ways:
  ✓   Click the Info button in the top menu button row.
  ✓   Use the File menu command /fi.
  ˙   Some songs may also open it automatically when the song is loaded.

The page has two tabs:
  ✓   Info shows the stored content as rendered HTML.
  ✓   Edit shows the raw HTML in a textarea, so you can type or change it directly.

To close Songfile Info, click Close or press ESC.

There is no separate Save dialog. Changes are kept when you:
  ✓   leave the textarea,
  ✓   switch away from the Edit tab,
  ✓   change the "show on open" selector,
  ✓   or click the little Save checkmark button.

Allowed HTML is intentionally limited to simple formatting. Think of it as safe document markup such as:
  ✓   paragraphs and line breaks,
  ✓   headings,
  ✓   lists,
  ✓   emphasis such as bold and italics,
  ✓   code/preformatted text,
  ✓   and simple tables.
  ˙   Scripts, links, forms, images, styles, and other active or external content are stripped out.

The show on open selector has three meanings:
  ✓   none means do not automatically show Songfile Info when the song opens.
  ✓   parked means open it in its normal parked place in the app.
  ✓   float means open it as a floating window.

If the Songfile Info content is empty, the app treats that as no Info and will not auto-open it, even if the selector had been set earlier.

Map Spacebar

You can map the spacebar to one transport action at a time. Use the File menu path /fm to choose what the spacebar should do.

The top-level spacebar mappings are:

  • Restart song: /fmR
  • Restart section: /fmr
  • Reset song: /fmz
  • Reset song hard: /fmZ
  • Unset the spacebar mapping: /fmu

You can also map the spacebar to section navigation with /fms or beat navigation with /fmb.

Once mapped, pressing SPACE runs that transport action directly. This is useful for hands-on practice when you want one repeatable motion under your picking hand or your foot pedal keyboard.

Reset song returns you to Section 1, Beat 1 and preserves the active loop mode if one was already running. Reset song hard does the same repositioning, but also triggers the stronger reset behavior used by plugins that need to clear generated state.

Section Destination


Destination § is a dropdown that gets updated as you navigate Sections. This is the Destination Section number of Move, Add, Shallow, and Clone. There are some subtle differences.

When moving Sections, pay attention to the Destination. It's just like a deck of cards: if you pull a card out of the middle, and put it in earlier or later in the stack, the cardinal number of the cards changes. This is where the moved Section ends up.

  • So chosing Destination of 3 moves the current Section to Section 3, and Section 3 becomes Section 4, Section 4 becomes Section 5, and so on to the end of the Song. Also the Sections after the current Section get moved towards the beginning of the Song to close the gap.
  • Chosing "BEGIN" as the Destination moves to Section 1, pushing the existing Section 1 to Section 2, and bumping all other Sections later as well,
  • Chosing "END" as the Destination moves the current Section to the end, after all existing Sections, the Sections after the current Section being moved towards the beginning of the Song to close the gap.

When Adding or Cloning Sections, new Section goes after the Destination which gets updated as you navigate Sections. However, if you change the dropdown, the new Section will go after that Section number. This is where they end up:

  • Add: creates a new Section after the current Section (or Section shown in Destination), bumping any existing later Sections later in the Song.
  • Shallow, and Clone: clones the current Section, adding it after the existing Section (or Section shown in Destination), bumping existing Sections.
  • Delete: deletes the current Section, (ignoring Destination), moving any following Sections towards the beginning of the Song to close the gap.

Transposing

Transposing "up" moves the notes and the Root Key up the scale one half-step. (Uppercase matters: u goes up, U goes down. d goes down, D goes up.) Transposing with the "j" key ("jump") moves the notes and the Root up a fourth, which jumps up one string on a P4 instrument. Remember that jumping up a fourth from the G string to the B string in Standard guitar tuning will not be on the same fret. (Uppercase matters: j jumps up, J jumps down.)

The keyboard shortcut for changing the key is "k", which cycles through the keys in chromatic order, so that you can quickly try out different keys. This allows you to see which key makes the most sense for the notes chosen. You will see the function names (and auto-colors) change as you change keys, since the Note Functions are relative to the Root Key. "Shift-k" cycles through the keys in reverse chromatic order. The new key root is shown in the caption bar of the Section and the caption bar of the instrument, and all the notes that match the key letter are highlighted until you do something else.

The "Sharps" and "Flats" buttons in the Section menu let you choose whether to show the notes on the neck with sharps or flats. This is just a display option, and does not change the actual notes on the neck, which are always the same MIDI pitches. So if you have a C# note, it will be shown as C# if you choose "Sharps", and as D♭ if you choose "Flats". The command-line shortcut for doing this is /ss, for section | sharps, or /sf, for section | flats. [After any command-line command, hit ESC to close the command-line.]

Just like using the dropdown "Key" or "LeadKey" on the Section menu page, on the command-line you can also set the key of a Section with the command /sk. This brings you to the menus choices of white keys where you can choose the keys: a, b, c, d, e, f, g, which sets the Root key, and sets Sharps to true for the major keys A,B,C,D,E and G which have sharps, and sets Flats to true for the major key of F, which has a B♭.

At that /sk menu, you could also select black keys where you can choose the keys: a♭, b♭, d♭, e♭, g♭, because these major keys have flats. You can then override Sharps or Flats with /ss, or /sf.

So choosing "e♭" would set the Root key to E♭ and Flats to true. If you then chose Sharps, the Root key would be D♯ and all other black keys would be shown as sharps.

LeadKey is only displayed on the neck using TinyNotes, and these show only the Note Function, not the Note Name. So just set the LeadKey dropdown to the pitch you will be playing, even though its enharmonic name may be incorrect, because whether Sharps or Flats are shown in the LeadKey dropdown is chosen by the Section's Sharps/Flats buttons, which also set the choices displayed for the Key dropdown. Similarly, using the command-line to set LeadKey, choose the correct pitch using the available "black keys" menu choice, e.g. D♭ instead of C♯, which is not available through the command-line.

You would select G as the Root key, and select Flats. infinite-neck doesn't know about major and minor keys or key signatures. It just lets you display all the black keys with either Flats names or Sharps names. No mixed use of sharps and flats is displayed, there is no concept of accidentals, there are no double flats or double sharps, and there is no display of enharmonic names like C♭ B♯ E♯ or F♭.

Palette

The Palette lets you choose what kind of note you'll be placing when you click in a cell, and what color and hatching will be used.

First, choose what kind of note: Named, Single, Tiny, Bend, Pitch Highlight, Multi Highlight, or Fingering. There's more detail in the Glossary about these note kinds.

Then, decide whether you'll use AutoColor. With AutoColor, Note Functions are assigned a color each by the current ColorScheme. Without AutoColor, more color buttons will appear: these are colors assigned to Roles. A Role is something like Root or Chord. All Root notes then have the same color. All Chord notes have the same color. (Except that we provide not just one but [Chord, Ch2, and Ch3], so that you can have three different chord colors to be assigned as you click.) If you just want a color for each note, that you'll assign, then click "More..." after the Major Seventh role color radio button. You'll then see the full palette infinite-neck provides. Whenever AutoColor is active, your color choices for individual colors chosen this way will be ignored.

AutoColor uses a ColorScheme. infinite-neck comes with a Default ColorScheme, called "CycleOfColors", which is based on my whack theory that associating a color with each Note Function helps to learn and spot patterns of chords. That is, a Major Seventh chord always looks the same, color-wise, so seeing an inversion of that chord on the neck should stimulate a memory, and help us navigate the pattern of that chord all around the neck. If you find it weird or annoying, you have two options: 1) turn AutoColor off; 2) customize AutoColor to a scheme of your liking.

Customization also allows you to have multiple ColorSchemes, for example, one scheme for minor moods, another for major moods.

With AutoColor off, you can still have the colors and hatches mean something, by using Roles. A Role for Root or Chord was discussed above. You can also use or customize the Roles for Avoid, Avoid2 and Avoid3, so that these notes mean "Avoid this note!" To do this, you would turn AutoColor off, then click Avoid, then click in a cell to place that Avoid note, either a SingleNote, or a NamedNote. You can do this in multiple keys, such that in Section 1, in the key of C, the flat II (τ) would be an Avoid note, while in Section 2, in the key of A playing a Gypsy scale where the flat 2 (τ) is legal, then the natural 2 (II) would be an Avoid note, so you'd place a Role note of Avoid there.

Customizing the colors for the Roles [Root, Chord, and Scale] sets these as the colors for the Fill menu. When using Fill, you probably want AutoColor off to take advantage of this.

To change ColorScheme away from CycleOfColors, click the [Customize...] button on the Palette menu. Chose colors and hatches for each of the Note Functions [note1, note2, ....,note12] using the little picker buttons and their pop-up pickers. (Click the little button again to make the picker go away, or click a color/hatch to chose it and make the picker go away.) When you have colors selected for each of the Note Functions [note1,...,note12], then click the [Record Stylesheet] button. You can provide a Stylesheet name, or the name "user" will be filled in for you. Now the Stylesheets will be shown, the same as if you had clicked the [Stylesheets] button, and your new Stylesheet will be a the bottom of the list. The bottom row is the composite stylesheet of built-in stylesheets plus your user stylesheets, as selected with the links and checkboxes, which you can play around with. This result is your active ColorScheme, and is saved in the song. To apply it to other songs, open this song first, delete any Sections, then open the Song you want to view using the "Append sections to current song" checkbox on the File menu. (There always has to be one Section, so just mash on the Delete Section button repeatedly in the Section menu until you are down to one Section and it is blank.) Now when you open the other song, you'll see that song's Sections, along with its saved Stylesheets, and your new Stylesheet.

You can also customize the colors for Fingering the same way. You'll see that the Default stylesheets are broken up into CycleOfColors for Note Functions, Fingerings for fingering colors, and Roles for Role colors. You may overide any of the individual colors in your stylesheet, and leave some Roles or Fingers or Note Functions as Default by not adding things in your stylesheet. You may also have multiple stylesheets by entering different names in "Stylesheet name" before clicking "Record Stylesheet".

There is another way to record a stylesheet: manually place colored NamedNotes on the neck, then click Section:NamedNotes==>Stylesheet. This will create a stylesheet for the Note Functions only, based on the colors you chose for those Note Function locations. You do not need to do all Note Functions--you could just override the II and the Δ for example. Then, when AutoColor is on the two notes in your stylesheet would override the Default, but other notes would use the Default.

Finally, there are several special "Color" buttons: [Clear, Keep, and Find Color].

"Keep" disables placing new notes, so is useful if you just want to click around and not change anything, or if you are doing a presentation and don't want to mess things up.

"Clear" lets you delete notes of the kind selected. Actually, you can also just click on a note, and if you have the note kind selected, that note will go away. If you click again, the note will come back, but with whatever color you currently have active.

To find what color you used before, make sure AutoColor is off, then click "Find Color", then click the note on the neck. The color name will appear in the Caption of that instrument, so open up the Caption row with the little hamburger on the instrument. If the whole Caption on the instrument is missing, press ESC then SHIFT-A to show it. The color will also be highlighted on the Palette, so you will have to have AutoColor off, and you should click "More..." after the [Δ] radio button to show the full color palette.

Two more colors are special: [Emboss, and Auto]. Emboss just creates a note with transparent background. This is useful if you'll be using AutoColor, so as to be obvious when AutoColor is off--all the notes will just be transparent but still have borders, highlights, etc. Emboss is also useful if you'd like to just display the Named Notes so you can see their positions all the way up the neck, and then use color to draw your attention to SingleNotes that you'll actually play.

The color called "Auto" places notes with a color selected by AutoColor, but that color persists even when AutoColor is off. This is useful if you want the colors from a ColorScheme to apply while placing notes, but you don't want to use AutoColor because you are using color Roles, or are using Emboss.

Event Wiring Controls

infinite-neck sends and recieves events on it's own internal EventBus, in its internal replay() loop, and (some day) on the MIDI bus. The Event Wiring Controls (toggle with keyboard shortcut e or with the "Wiring" buttons) let you map these events to each instrument. Here are the main controls:

  • Relative Section Amount: a text-string with a direction and an integer, giving a number of Sections to "look-ahead" or "look-behind" for notes to display in this table.
    • The default is the empty string, which means "Current Section".
    • Going +1 (ahead) means look-ahead to the next Section and show those notes in this table/instrument, so that this table is a preview of the coming Section.
    • Going -1 (behind) means look-behind to the previous Section and show what you just played.
    • In this way, you can place a look-behind instrument just above your instrument, and a look-ahead instrument just below your instrument, and you have a three paned view of (1) past notes, (2) notes in this Section, and (3) notes coming up, otherwise known as a "Piano Roll" or similar to what you coud see in a score, or chart, or Guitar-Hero, or Synthesia, or Tetris.
  • Instrument: a dropdown list of other instruments, so you can "listen" to their NamedNotes, PlayedNotes, RecordedNotes, and Highlights.
    • Notes you "listen" to will be replayed in your instrument but not recorded in your instrument .
    • This is useful if you want to see the notes of a standard tuning song on the neck of your P4 turning, for example.
    • You can only listen to one instrument, and you can't listen to your own instrument.
    • If you chose "Relative Section" in a clone of your instrument, you can have the clones listen to your instrument, in which case you have look-ahead and look-behind of other Sections.
  • Wiring button: shows various states: [No Instrument], [Add Wiring], [Wired].
    • [No Instrument] means you need to select an instrument from the Instrument drop-down. If it only shows "none" you need to add one more instrument into My Tunings from the All Tunings library.
    • [Add Wiring] means you are ready to wire the selected instruments events to the current instrument, possibly with Relative Section Amount. Clicking on the button wires the events, and puts you in the [Wired] state for that instrument.
    • [Wired] means you are actively listening to another instrument in this Section, or in another Section with Relative Section Amount. Change either Relative Section Amount or selected Instrument to have the button go to [Add Wiring] state so you can change the wiring.

Options

Beware that keyboard shortcuts may be captured by textboxes such as the song name or BPM, or even the radio buttons of colors on the Palette. Hit ESC to close up menus to ensure the keyboard shortcuts documented here behave as expected.

Menu :: View :: Natural fret widths

When checked, pressing w or W while menus are closed will increase/decrease the width of the frets, but will use the natural mathematical proportion of frets, such that they get smaller up the neck. This is the default. When not checked, the w and W keys still increase and decrease, but all frets have the same width.

Certain instruments in the library have an option in the JSON file called fixedFretWidthMult which scales the fixed width, and thus do not use Natural Fret Widths. Piano Schroeder is one of these.

Menu :: Section :: Allow transposition by keyboard shortcuts d, U, u, D,  j , & J

When checked, pressing u or D while menus are closed will transpose any named-notes up chromatically, preserving their role colors/styles. d or U transposes down. Pressing j or J will transpose by fourths, or Jump strings up or down. When not checked, these keys cannot trigger transposition, which is the safe default.

Command-line Overview

Here is the command-line interface, so you can type commands one-handed while balancing your guitar on your knee.

The menu is structured to give unique, quick access to many of the things you can do with the mouse. Many quick functions are available via the global keyboard shortcuts. The global keyboard shortcuts plus the command-line commands together give you access to the options of infinite-neck you need when working in a flow and don't want to be digging around in menus.

The command-line menu commands don't change because they all start from the root of the menu. Any menu item that you use can just be typed again and again without even looking at the screen. All menus end with "x" so you can end any command with x and close the command-line. Most of the time you can also just type / in the command-menu to restart at the top. A few of the input dialogs in the menu will trap the / in which case you can hit ESC to exit the command-line.

There are also some advanced or diagnostic items that don't belong in the Graphical User Interface (GUI). As you get more familiar with using infinite-neck, please check this reference, or poke around in the command-line menu to learn some of these advanced features. A few, like the Display Options dump, or the Sections dump, are essential, and can't be launched from the GUI. Output from these commands often appears at the bottom of the main page, below any instruments, so you'll have to hit ESC to hide the command-line menu and scroll down to see them.

Plugins

Overview

The Plugins menu adds practice and automation tools that operate on the current Song, current Section, beats, keys, and visible instruments.

The current plugins are:

  • ArpeggioPlugin
  • FillPlugin
  • MovePlugin
  • TransposePlugin

In the command-line menu, plugins live under /fpfile plugins . Each plugin also has a hhelp action that shows its current settings and the events it handles.

ArpeggioPlugin

What It Does

ArpeggioPlugin generates played-note prompts from the named notes already present in the current Section. It is used for drills, fret-range/neck-position practice, learning keys, and flashcard-style note recall.

Typical Use Cases

  • Practice one chord or arpeggio shape in many keys without having to author a separate songfile for each key.
  • Limit practice to a small region of the neck by setting minFret and maxFret.
  • Hide all normal named notes on the neck, then let ArpeggioPlugin reveal only the current notes you want to practice.
  • Use flashcard mode so you guess first, then see the answer on the next beat.
  • Combine ArpeggioPlugin with TransposePlugin so one Song becomes a multi-key practice tool or game.

Main Options

  • m minFret: lowest fret considered by the generated pattern.
  • t maxFret: highest fret considered by the generated pattern.
  • o lowToHigh: control whether the candidate traversal starts from lower strings and lower frets or the reverse logical order.
  • u upOnly: keep the pattern moving upward instead of bouncing back.
  • s style chooses every, alternate, random, or bach.
  • n show note name chooses off, one, all, or played.
  • c color notes chooses Emboss or AutoColor-style.
  • f flashcard delays note-name reveal by one beat.

Show Note Name Modes

  • off: show only highlights, with no note-name text.
  • one: show only one beat's note-name text at a time.
  • all: show note-name text for all generated beats in the current Section.
  • played: accumulate note-name text beat by beat as the pass continues.

Flashcard Mode

Flashcard mode changes the timing of note-name reveal, not the highlight itself.

  • On beat 1 you see the highlight only.
  • You guess, say the name or function out loud, and play the note on your physical instrument.
  • On the next beat, the previous note's information is revealed.
  • On the final beat, the current note is also revealed because there is no later beat to show it.
  • This ties visually seeing the note position with hearing the note name and training your body memory to play that note along with hearing or seeing the note name or function.

This creates a simple rhythm for practice:

  • see the highlight
  • guess and play
  • see the answer on the next beat

Practical Practice Flows

Highlight Only

Set n show note name to off. This is the most demanding mode. You see fretboard position only.

Embossed Prompt Notes

Hide normal named notes through the View controls, then set Arpeggio's note-name mode to one, all, or played with c color notes off. The generated prompts stand out from the rest of the neck without using the full AutoColor palette.

AutoColored Prompt Notes

Leave the rest of the neck visually plain or hidden, then set c color notes on. Arpeggio's prompt notes will use the current AutoColor mapping and can stand out strongly inside the selected fret range.

Flashcard Drill

Set f flashcard on and choose a note-name mode. This is useful when you want a short delay between the guess and the reveal.

Actions

  • A Apply regenerates the current Section immediately.
  • C Clear removes Arpeggio-generated notes without touching user-authored notes.
  • h help shows a summary of current settings.

Events

  • When enabled, ArpeggioPlugin handles DaCapo:OnSectionBegin.

Example Command-Line Flow

One possible command-line rhythm is:

  • p plugins
  • a arpeggio
  • m minFret and t maxFret
  • n show note name
  • c color notes
  • A Apply

FillPlugin

What It Does

FillPlugin writes plugin-owned notes into the current Section and selected table. It can fill NamedNotes, SingleNotes, and TinyNotes from the current Section root, chord formula, and scale formula.

NamedNote ignores fret and string limits. SingleNote and standalone TinyNote obey the configured fret and string limits. SingleNote can also add an overlay TinyNote in the same cell, but that overlay is ignored whenever standalone TinyNote is active and emits notes.

Main Options

  • t target table chooses the main table to fill. By default it is the first table that is not wired to display another table.
  • c chord formula and g scale formula choose the note sets to use with the current Section root.
  • i minFret and a maxFret limit the fret range.
  • u upper string limit and l lower string limit limit the string range.
  • n NamedNote, s SingleNote, and t TinyNote each have root, chord, and scale submenus with none, keep, and role modes.
  • NamedNote and TinyNote include C Copy from SingleNote to copy the SingleNote root, chord, and scale settings once.
  • Fill pass order is scale, then chord, then root.
  • Inside SingleNote, a add TinyNote chooses none, emboss, noteAutomatic, noteLead, or noteLead2 for overlay Tiny companions.

Actions And Events

  • A Apply fills only the current Section in the selected table.
  • C Clear removes FillPlugin-owned notes from the current Section in the selected table.
  • S ClearSong removes FillPlugin-owned notes from all Sections in the selected table.
  • M Commit Notes removes FillPlugin ownership from generated notes in all Sections of the selected table.
  • h help shows a summary of current settings.
  • When enabled, FillPlugin handles DaCapo:OnSectionBegin and applies to the section that just began.

MovePlugin

What It Does

MovePlugin moves PlayedNotes and RecordedNotes inside the current Section and one selected target table (Instrument). It is used to shift chords or patterns up or down the neck, or "jump" them up or down strings, similar to how Transposition works. But Transposition only works on NamedNotes. (See: TransposePlugin which also transposes the whole Song with lots of options, specifically geared to transposing while looping, and only does NamedNotes currently.) With MovePlugin you can transpose PlayedNotes and RecordedNotes for SingleNotes, TinyNotes, and Highlights, individually or in groups, i.e. "all SingleNote PlayedNotes", or "all SingleNotes, TinyNotes and Highlights in RecordedNotes".

Named notes are not moved by this plugin.

Since you can move notes right off the neck in all directions, the plugin deals with this by giving you three "algorithms".

  • octave: Attempts to replace a shifted-off note by an octave down if you are going up, and and octave up if you are going down. Leads to unplayable chords such as two notes on one string, but they might still be played as arpeggios. So you can adjust the notes manually after moving (in the normal way: with the note type selected, click once on an existing note to clear it, then click once in a different cell to place a note.
  • string: Attempts to replace shifted off notes by wrapping them an octave down towards the bottom of the fretboard on the same string if you are going up, and towards the top of the fretboard if you are going down. For j jump up, and J jump down, wraps the chord or pattern around to the string on the opposite side of the neck.
  • drop: Does not attempt to replace notes, simply drops them.

Typical Use Cases

  • Move a phrase or drill up or down by one semitone with m motions set to u up or d down.
  • Jump a line to a neighboring string with j jump up or J jump down.
  • Move only PlayedNotes, only RecordedNotes, or both, by changing the i include toggles.
  • Reposition SingleNotes or Tiny notes in beat looping.
  • Reposition recorded Highlights in beat looping.
  • Try a move, inspect s show dropped notes, then use c clear/backup to arm a fresh graveyard backup before the next pass.

Main Options

  • T target table chooses which current-section instrument table to move.
  • a algorithm chooses d drop, o octave, or s string fallback when a direct landing is not available.
  • m motions chooses u up, d down, j jump up, or J jump down.
  • i include opens toggles for s single notes, t tiny notes and bends, h highlights, p played notes, and r recorded notes.
  • You must select one of played notes or recorded notes or both, plus at least one note type for anything to happen.
  • The include caption shows a summary such as include [s,t,p] so you can see the active filters at a glance.

Actions And Events

  • A Apply moves the selected note layers in the current Section and selected table.
  • s show dropped notes opens the current dropped-notes log as JSON in Messages.
  • c clear/backup clears the dropped-notes log and arms a fresh section backup before the next Apply. The backup is not put in the Graveyard until Apply is hit.
  • h help shows the current MovePlugin settings and summary.

Before the first Apply in a run, MovePlugin buries a copy of the current Section in the graveyard, which is a backup, but does not delete the Section. If a move lands badly or you simply want the older state back, revive that Section from the graveyard. The bury happens on Apply after selecting clear/backup.

MovePlugin does not handle DaCapo events, and so does nothing in response to looping. It runs only when you invoke its actions from the command-line menu. However, you can keep looping or beat looping while using Apply. In fact, this is the best way to see how it moves RecordedNotes, since only the first beat will render after an Apply if you aren't looping

TransposePlugin

What It Does

TransposePlugin moves one Song through a programmed interval cycle. The first interval is the starting state. Each Apply advances to the next interval and uses only the difference from the currently applied interval. Looping when the plugin is enabled applies the transposition every loop, at song begin. Since the first interval is always 0, the transposition is noticable on the second loop.

Main Options

  • i intervals is a JSON array of target intervals.
  • n named notes chooses whether named notes move.
  • a auto sharps/flats applies the same key spelling after transposition as found in the menu /sc (section key).
  • d do lead key also transposes the Section lead key when keys move.

TransposePlugin does not move PlayedNotes or RecordedNotes. Use MovePlugin when you want to move those layers deterministically.

Actions

  • A Apply advances to the next configured interval.
  • S Reset returns the plugin-managed transpose offset to zero.
  • h help shows current interval, offset, options, and handled events.

Events

  • When enabled, TransposePlugin handles DaCapo:OnSongEnd.

Typical Use Cases

  • Take one authored Song such as a chord or scale drill and practice it in all 12 keys.
  • Pair with ArpeggioPlugin so the same authored practice pattern can move across keys automatically.
  • Reduce maintenance of many nearly identical Song files that only differ by key.

Arpeggio And Transpose Together

The two plugins are especially useful together.

  • TransposePlugin changes the key progression over time.
  • ArpeggioPlugin regenerates and displays practice prompts inside the current fret range and display mode.
  • The user can choose whether the neck is fully labeled, mostly hidden, color-coded, or used in flashcard style.

Glossary

Term Definition
Note Names Names of notes in the octave: [A, A#, B, B#, ..., G#] or [A, B♭, ...]. See wikipedia's entry. In infinite-neck, each Section allows flats, or sharps, but not both. Also, infinite-neck does not display the enharmonic names such as B#, or C##.
Note Functions What a note functions as. For example, in the key of C, the note G functions as "the Five" (or "V"). The function is where a note sits in the mode or scale you are playing. So if the band switches to the key of A, that G note becomes the dominant seven, because we count from A up seven scale-tones. This would be the case if we were playing in A Mixolydian. If we were in A major, the mode has G# as the major seven, so the G is a flatted G#--that is, not in the major mode. infinite-neck deals with this by assigning a unique symbol to each Note Function: a dominant seven is δ and a major seven is Δ.
MIDI Number The MIDI specification assigns an integer to each pitch within a range from 0 to 127, a superset of the piano range. Here is a decent MIDI chart. Note that there are several conventions as to what to call the notes, e.g. C4 or C3 for middle-C. However, the MIDI number is constant. A middle-C is always MIDI Number 60. infinite-neck shows the MIDI Number, not the names such as C3, C4 or c``.
NamedNote Display of all notes with the same Note Name. Placing a NamedNote such as "F" on the neck marks all F's. A NamedNote is its own class of note, the three classes being NamedNotes, PlayedNotes, and RecordedNotes.
SingleNote Display of one note on the neck. Placing a SingleNote such as "F" on the neck marks one F -- the one you clicked. SingleNotes always float over NamedNotes, so you can add SingleNotes on top of NamedNotes and even use different colors if AutoColor is off. SingleNotes have one rounded corner so you can see if a NamedNote is present.
TinyNote A smaller note display that stays visible over NamedNotes and SingleNotes. (A Bend is a special type of TinyNote, so you can't use both in one cell.) TinyNotes (including Bends) are special in that they follow LeadKey (set on the SectionDrawer) in coloring and Function. If you have LeadKey set to G and Key set to C, the TinyNote at G will display Function I, but the NamedNote at G will display V. But if you have LeadKey set to "root", which is the default, then the TinyNote will display the Function V, the same as a NamedNote or SingleNote in that same cell.
Bend A special type of TinyNote, which shows the range of notes that bending (stretching) a string moves through. For example, if you finger a note at A, but then stretch the string sideways, it will raise the pitch smoothly through A#, G, and maybe even G# if you have light strings or are high up on the neck. infinite-neck designates the cell where your bend starts with a square end, and the destination note as the pointed end. In the same example, A is the start, A#, G and G# are the destination notes. A# would be a semitone bend, G would be a 2-semitone bend, and G# a 3-semitone bend. A pre-bend is where you bend the note up before plucking the string, and then release the tension so that the pitch drops--the destination is then A. infinite-neck calls it an "up-down" bend where you start off relaxed, sound the note, then bend up and then release the tension so the pitch comes back down.
PlayedNote Any SingleNote, TinyNote, or Bend that is placed when REC is not in effect, that is, when not recording, is a PlayedNote. NamedNotes are never PlayedNotes. Highlights cannot be PlayedNotes, only RecordedNotes. (But a Highlight will work when not recording for demonstration purposes and will simply not be saved. So if you are leading a presentation or class, you can highlight all notes with the same MIDI pitch number with Pitch, or can highlight multiple single notes with Multi. When you place any other kind of note or Highlight, or change Sections, your Highlight disappears.) Except for NamedNotes, any note placed while recording is a RecordedNote.
RecordedNote Any SingleNote, TinyNote, Bend, or Highlight (Pitch or Multi), that is placed when REC is in effect, that is, when recording, are RecordedNotes. NamedNotes are never RecordedNotes. Any note placed while not recording is either a NamedNote or a PlayedNote.
semitone A half-step, e.g. from C to C#. A semitone Bend is where you stretch the string so that the pitch that sounds is a half-step (semitone) above where the fretted pitch would be if played relaxed.
pre-bend A pre-bend is where you bend the note up before plucking the string, and then release the tension so that the pitch drops.
up-down infinite-neck calls it an "up-down" bend where you start off relaxed, sound the note, then bend up and then release the tension so the pitch comes back down.
Fingering Which finger you use to play each note: 1, 2, 3, 4, T correspond to Index finger, Middle finger, Ring finger, Pinkie, and Thumb. The special finger "o" is reserved for marking a string open. It is customary to place this "fingering" on the cells behind the nut, since these cells represent the notes that sound when playing an open string. It is the letter o, not the number zero, since 0 is used as the global keyboard shortcut to change the width of the nut. Also, "Fingering" can means simply placing a finger on a string at a fret.
Section Corresponds roughly to a Bar of music. Except that if the groove and key stay the same, you might have multiple Bars for the Section. infinite-neck does not distinguish or show Bars, just Beats in Sections. If you want to show 4 bars of groove in G, but each bar should show 4 beats so that you can show chords that change on the beat, then you'd create a Section with 16 beats. This would be useful if you wanted to show the "fabric" of the groove with NamedNotes, and then use SingleNotes to show actual notes to play.

If you had a basic 12-Bar Blues, ( C|C|C|C / F|F|C|C / G|F|C|G ), you could model it as one Section of 16 beats, then two sections of 8 beats each, then 4 Sections of 4 beats each.

In a Funk jam, there are typically two Sections--when the bass player changes the Root, that would be the second Section, and when the Bass player goes back, we'd be back in the first section, regardless of whether we hung on the first Root for 8 bars. Alternately, some jams have two separate bass Root notes per bar, while the mode stays basically the same. These can be modeled as two Sections per bar, or as one Section, and then use RecordedNotes to show the bass moving.

When you are placing notes in infinite-neck, you are looking at the neck in one Section. Within this section, as the Beats roll by, SingleNotes, TinyNotes, and Highlights can move around, but everything else stays the same. Specifically, the NamedNotes keep their positions for the entire Section. In one Section, the things that stay the same are: Caption, Root Key, Lead Root Key, Display Options, Theme, and the enharmonic display of key (A# versus G♭). When you go to the next Section, you get a new collection of recorded notes in Beats. That is, Section 1 could have two beats with up to 2 sets of recorded notes; Section 2 could have 16 beats with up to 16 sets of recorded notes.
Beat One display of notes within a Section. At every beat change, you can change SingleNotes, TinyNotes, and Highlights, but you don't have to. To change these on each Beat, record (press "Rec"), then click the notes you want, then change to the next Beat with the Transport or keyboard shortcut without changing Section. When done, turn off recording. You can then Loop Beats, or Loop the Song, to see your work. You can place notes while looping, but you gotta be fast, or set the BPM to be slow, while editing. A Beat is associated with the BPM (Beat Per Minute). So at 80 BPM, you'll get 80 beats in one minute. If you had 8 beats to Section 1, and 8 beats to Section 2, looping over these two Sections would yeild 5 loops in one minute. Each Section may have different number of Beats. If you are matching a song in 4/4 at 80 BPM, then put 4 Beats in each Section. If you want to show fingering on the 1/8th note, put 8 Beats per Section, and set BPM to 160.
Instrument In infinite-neck, an Instrument is one display of the neck. Even though in the real world, while a single instrument can be re-tuned to another Tuning, it would still be the same instrument, e.g. "my Flame Gibson Guitar." In infinite-neck when you have a 6-string guitar in DADGAD displayed on the same page as a 6-string guitar in Standard tuning, we call it two Instruments.
Tunings Each set of pitches on one instrument is called a Tuning. So for 6-string guitar, DADGAD would be one Tuning, P4 would be another Tuning. Banjo Graveyard is another Tuning, as is Banjo Sawmill. Each instrument displayed in infinite-neck is one Tuning, but you can display multiple instruments.
Special Rows (SR) In Tunings, "Special Rows" checkboxes, showed as a column "SR" in the tables "My Tunings", or the "All Tunings" library, cause the special rows of the instrument to be shown in different background colors, to distinguish them from P4 tunings. For Standard tuning 6-string guitar, the "Special Rows" are the first two rows. See the instructions Help: Special Rows topic to see how to change it.
Diamond Rows In "My Tunings", or the "All Tunings" library, the column "Diamonds" checkmark causes the diamonds found on the necks of real instruments to be shown below the fretboard in infinite-neck. Diamonds are the dots, squares, diamonds, or other shapes and inlays (in pearl, ebony etc.) found typically at the 3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th, and twelth frets on the fretboard of guitars, and sometimes on the top edge of the neck, too. Each Tuning in the Tunings Library may have "diamonds" property and a "doubleDiamonds" property which give the fret locations for instruments that have them (e.g. a Uke has different positions than guitars, and some guitars have diamonds on the first fret while many don't). DoubleDiamonds on guitars usually mark the twelth fret (octave). Instruments such as Cellos don't have them in infinite-neck. Diamonds and DoubleDiamonds are styled differently in Themes.
Banjo Nut (BN) In "My Tunings", or the "All Tunings" library, the values in column "BN" causes the Nut location to move per string. Used for modelling Banjo, and also for creating custom Capos in USER tunings. See the instructions under "Customize USER Tuning" in the "All Tunings" library at the bottom of the table.
Root The note at the first degree of a scale or mode. Also, the Root Key (or Key of the Root Note). So in A minor, A is the Root and the Root Key.
Root/Chord/Scale - in Fill Root would be the home note of the key of the bass player, Chord is the chord you want to play in this key, and Scale is the collection of notes that define that key or mode. Fill allows you to color these in different colors. So you could play in C, playing [C,D,E,F,G,A,B,C] and these would be the scale notes for Cmajor, with a Root of C. If you played [C,D,E,F,G,A,B♭,C] you could say you were playing C Mixolydian, so C is the Root, and these notes are the Scale notes. An appropriate Chord would then be C7 (C dominant 7). infinite-neck allows you to Fill the neck with NamedNotes using Chords and Scale notes (including modes) that are not necessarily in the key. So if you set the key to C, then fill with a m7b5 chord and Locrian scale, you'll notice that the m7b5 chord fits over the Locrian mode scale notes, but that most of these are not in C major scale. So really, you aren't in C major, but in C Locrian, and it works perfectly with a C m7b5. These are all available in the Fill menu dropdowns.
Cell One spot on the neck -- one string at one fret. The term comes from spreadsheets, since we use HTML tables to model the fretboard.
Theme A collection of styles, shapes, and sizes for the display of the instrument neck. There are built-in Themes that you can select for a song using the dropdown in the main menu row of buttons. (Or, you can select different Themes for any Section if you save the DisplayOptions for that Section.) You can tweak the Theme to your liking using the Themes Menu. Advanced users can define their own by editing the JSON text file themes.js.
Highlight Highlights are note types that come in two flavors: Multi and Pitch. They highlight a note or set of notes with a bright glowing border.
Pitch 1) A setting for Highlight that displays a Highlight around all notes with the same MIDI pitch on the Instrument. Since a middle C appears in multiple spots across strings on a guitar, for example, clicking Pitch as the note type, then clicking a C will highlight all C's on the neck with that same MIDI number. Middle C with MIDI number 60 appears on a Standard tuning 6-string guitar on the second string, first fret; on the third string, fifth fret; on the fourth string, tenth fret; and on the fifth string, thirteenth fret. These would all be highlighted. But other C notes with different MIDI numbers would not be highlighted. For example, the C with MIDI number 48 at the fifth string, third fret would not be highlighted. Pitch can also be used to highlight notes for demonstration purposes when not recording. Click a note and a Highlight will appear on each note on the neck that shares MIDI pitch number, but will disappear when you navigate Sections or place another note type.

2) A specific frequency of note, e.g. a 440Hz Concert A. The MIDI spec defines default frequencies of pitch for each named note. In infinite-neck we simply use one MIDI number for each note, so pitches here are standardized on the MIDI default pitch spec, and we allow display of the MIDI pitch number for each cell. That is, we don't show microtonal pitches, or alternative pitch-tunings.
Multi Highlight style that lets you highlight multiple notes on one Beat. Multi can also be used to highlight multiple notes for demonstration purposes when not recording. Click the notes and a highlight will appear on each, but will disappear when you navigate Sections or place another note type.
Display Options Each Section can remember the options set on the View menu, when you click Save Display Options on the Section or View menu pages.

Some size options are not automated on playback if Presentation Mode is not checked. [Cell width, Cell height, NoteFontSize]. The idea is that changing the cell widths while looping would be annoying, but if you were presenting in a classroom, you might want to "zoom in" on a portion of the neck, so you'd change the cell width for that one Section, say, Section 2. Then, when you went from Section 1, where you show all possible notes for C Mixolydian, to Section 2, where you show just the first 5 frets on screen. You can set this all up in advance, so that in your presentation, all you need to do is hit the > key to advance through the example.

When these get out of control or confusing, audit them with the keyboard command-line /vdd Scroll down to see the audit of all Sections with Display Options. The options that change are highlighted. You can also sit on the View page and watch the controls change while you hit the Transport next section buttons, or the keyboard shortcuts ( < and > ). Remember that if one of the controls traps the keyboard, click in some blank spot of the screen or hit ESC and then V to come back to the View menu.

See more info in the Display Options context help topic.

Clone 		Sections may be Cloned with either "Clone" or "Shallow Clone". "Clone" copies everything about the Section, including all Notes.
Shallow Clone Sections may be Cloned with either "Clone" or "Shallow Clone". "Shallow Clone" copies everything about the Section, but only NamedNotes, not SingleNotes or TinyNotes. This is useful if you want to stay in the same key and groove, but show different chords or fingerings in a new Section.
