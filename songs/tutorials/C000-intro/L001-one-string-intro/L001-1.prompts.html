Intro to stringed instruments: playing on one string

Getting Started: Chromatic Scale

You are going to play the Chromatic scale on the E string, which is the fattest string on the 4-string bass and the guitar.

In the next step, we'll guide you through the notes. In this step, just look at the notes so you can see that each fret has a circled number next to it. These are where your fingers go, when you play them one-by-one. These are "Fingering" notes, and show which finger you should use for each fret, 1 being your Index finger, 2 being your middle finger, 3 being your ring finger, and 4 being your pinkie. Go to the Next step, where you can loop the notes and play along.

Scale in E Major

Now on the E string, which is the fattest string on the 4-string bass or the guitar, play the E Major scale. This is the first, and lowest, major scale you can play on the guitar. Major scales sound bright and happy, like a march or a band song, and like a lot of church hymns. Many children's songs are also in major scales.

Scale in A Minor

Now, on the A string, which is the second-fattest string on the bass or guitar, we are going to play an A natural minor scale. This scale is useful for playing the Blues. We could also play this scale on the E string, or any string, but changing it to the A string starts the scale in a different place than you've just been practicing with, so we want to have you retain the E major sound as the first part of a song, and the A minor sounds as the second part of the same song. Later we'll play them together in a progression, and you'll follow the changes in sounds and moods and recognized them from your practice, and recognized these changes from other songs you have heard..

Minor scales are said to be "darker", or more melancholic, or less strident than major scales. In fact many major and minor scales use the same notes but have different relative starting places, so the happy/sad distinction is fairly arbitrary, and many songs, like Blues songs, go back and forth between minor and major fluidly, as a way to change up the mood. Listen for the changes and think of songs they remind you of while you play. Since it sounds too "muddy" to play all the notes at once, scales are a way to tell a story with the notes and weave a journey into the song, while implying chords and "fabrics" of note collections that bring the distinctive sounds to different songs.
