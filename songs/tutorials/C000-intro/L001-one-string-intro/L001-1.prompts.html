Intro to stringed instruments: playing on one string

Getting Started: Chromatic Scale

Play the Chromatic scale on the E string, which is the fattest string on the 4-string bass and the guitar.

Play each of the notes you see a circle with a number in it. These are Fingering notes, and show which finger you should use for each fret, 1 being your Index finger, 2 being your middle finger, 3 being your ring finger, and 4 being your pinkie.

Scale in E Major

Now on the E string, which is the fattest string on the 4-string bass or the guitar, play the E Major scale. This is the first, and lowest, major scale you can play on the guitar. Major scales sound bright and happy, like a march or a band song, and like a lot of church hymns. Many children's songs are also in major scales.

Scale in A Minor

Now, on the A string, which is the second-fattest string on the bass or guitar, we are going to play an A natural minor scale. This scale is useful for playing the Blues.

Minor scales are said to be "darker", or more melancholic, or less strident than major scales. In fact many major and minor scales use the same notes but have different relative starting places, so the happy/sad distinction is fairly arbitrary, and many songs, like Blues songs, go back and forth between minor and major fluidly, as a way to change up the mood. Listen for the changes and think of songs they remind you of while you play. Since it sounds too "muddy" to play all the notes at once, scales are a way to tell a story with the notes and weave a journey into the song, while implying chords and "fabrics" of note collections that bring the distinctive sounds to different songs.
